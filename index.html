A Large-scale Dataset for Global Dynamic Emotion Description in Dialogue Speech

Abstract

Understanding empathetic multi-turn dialogues requires accurately tracking complex emotional dynamics and capturing rich paralinguistic cues. However, existing datasets predominantly offer discrete sentence-level emotion labels (e.g., MELD) or coarse text-only summaries (e.g., SAMSum), severely limiting Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) in long-context emotional reasoning. To bridge this gap, we propose GDED, the first large-scale multi-turn speech dataset featuring fine-grained natural language descriptions of dynamic emotional evolution and paralinguistic attributes.To efficiently construct GDED, we design a novel three-stage automated annotation pipeline. At its core, we propose a dynamic emotion recognition framework that integrates prompt engineering, the psychological "Peak-End Rule," and a coarse-to-fine (C2F) training strategy. Specifically, it leverages acoustic arousal to generate Causal Prompts and Trend Prompts, effectively guiding models to capture both micro-level emotion triggers and macro-level emotional shifts while alleviating context degradation. Furthermore, we extract and discretize frame-level acoustic and demographic features, ultimately fusing these multi-dimensional representations into expressive, paragraph-level captions via a Llama3-8B fine-tuned on high-quality human seed data.The resulting bilingual dataset comprises 30,730 dialogues (595 hours) and maps emotional trajectories into seven classic "Emotional Arcs" based on narrative theory. Extensive evaluations demonstrate that our annotation pipeline significantly outperforms state-of-the-art MLLMs (e.g., Qwen-Audio, Audio-Flamingo3). Moreover, downstream experiments confirm that GDED provides crucial dynamic emotional priors, substantially enhancing Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) and Speech Emotion Captioning (SECap) systems.

Fine-grained Description

A young woman and her friend, both youths, engage in a heartfelt conversation about Speaker0s struggle with motivation to play the piano after facing harsh criticism from her family. The dialogue begins with Speaker 0 expressing disgust—her voice high-pitched but low in energy—as she vents her frustrations, while Speaker 1 responds neutrally, speaking in a low pitch with very little energy and a measured pace, offering quiet empathy without judgment. As the exchange continues, Speaker 0s tone shifts dramatically to one of surprise—first with medium speech rate and then slowing further—as her pitch drops low and her energy remains subdued, revealing her growing astonishment at something unexpected her friend has shared. Meanwhile, Speaker 1 maintains her calm neutrality throughout, though her consistently low pitch and minimal energy underscore deep care rather than detachment. By the final line, Speaker 0 speaks in a very low pitch and energy, yet her emotion is still labeled surprise, suggesting a dawning realization or shift in perspective. This evolution reflects a transformation in the emotional atmosphere: what starts as a disheartened, frustrated exchange gradually gives way to renewed hope, sparked by Speaker 1s gentle reminders of the joy and passion that once fueled Speaker 0s connection to music—and ultimately reawakens her resolve to reclaim that love despite familial opposition.
